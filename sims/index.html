Watch the patterns run

Every playbook in the library, animated. Each one shows who is allowed to speak, which agent is allowed to change the work, what gets sent, and when the document actually changes. The messages you see moving between the agents are the real ones from the playbooks, so the picture and the writing stay in step.

A run starts with a card describing a sample problem: who is in the room, what you need before you begin, what you get at the end, and whether agents are allowed to join or leave part-way through. It finishes with a card showing what actually came out. Use ◀ and ▶ to step through one at a time, or click any mark on the bar to jump.

the grammar

One vocabulary, fourteen simulations

Every simulation uses the same colours, the same markings and the same cast, so you only have to learn them once. The colour tells you what kind of thing something is. The ring, the dashes and the fading tell you whether it is allowed to speak.

Who is who

Chair — the agent running things. It opens the meeting, hands out turns and closes it, and never produces any of the work itself.
The pen — the one agent allowed to change the work, or to make the decision. Only amber arrows ever reach the document.
Peer — everyone else doing the work: reviewing, writing, suggesting changes, or sending in material.
Object — a thing rather than an agent: the document being worked on, the shared list of jobs, the agreed requirements.

May it speak

Idle — in the room and allowed to speak, but not speaking.
Holds the floor — it is this agent’s turn right now.
Muted — not allowed to speak at the moment, either for the whole meeting or until somebody uses its name.
Working privately — deliberately saying nothing while it works. Some patterns depend on this, so the agents cannot influence each other.
Absent — invited but never turned up, turned the job down, or was removed from the meeting.
Membership is a separate question: whether an agent can take on a job here at all. Each opening card says whether agents may join or leave freely, only between rounds, or not at all.

What is on the wire

Broadcast — something said to the whole room, such as the rules, the opening agreement, or the closing summary.
Directed — aimed at one agent or at one thing: a review, a suggested change, a question, taking a job off the list.
A change to the work — the only kind of message that produces a new version. The document flashes and its version number moves on.
A decision — accepting something, turning it down, picking a winner, or declaring the work finished.

Reading a run

The room is the box everything happens inside. Its name is in the corner.
The transcript on the right fills in as the run goes. It is the same record the playbook prints as its worked example.
The caption underneath explains what is happening and why the pattern works that way.
Most runs start by writing down what was agreed and end by writing down what happened, so a reader can tell where one piece of work stopped.
The first and last steps are cards rather than animation. One sets up the problem, the other shows what came out of it.
